# Practice questions

Forty scenario questions in the style of the exam, spread across the four domains in roughly their exam weights. They are written for this guide, not taken from the exam or from any book. Answer first, then check the key at the bottom, which says why each answer is right and points back to the section that covers it.

Read for the word that decides it: *first*, *best*, *next*, *most likely*, *except*. Where a question says **choose two**, both answers are needed.

### 1.0 Project Management Concepts

**1.** A predictive (waterfall) project is halfway through execution and the scope baseline has been approved. A stakeholder stops the PM in the hallway and asks for "one small addition" that the team could finish in a day. What should the PM do?

- A. Ask the stakeholder to submit a change request so its impact can be assessed
- B. Add it now, because it is small and keeps the stakeholder happy
- C. Refuse it outright, because an approved baseline can never be changed
- D. Add it and absorb the time into the contingency reserve for risks

**2.** A change request has been received and entered in the change control log. Following the standard sequence, what is the next step?

- A. Implement the change
- B. Conduct a preliminary review
- C. Update the baseline
- D. Escalate to the change control board

**3.** The vendor delivered the servers a week late, and testing has already slipped as a result. Where should this be recorded?

- A. The risk register
- B. The lessons learned register
- C. The issue log
- D. The change control log

**4.** The project team buys insurance to cover damage to the data center during the migration. Which risk response is this?

- A. Avoid
- B. Mitigate
- C. Accept
- D. Transfer

**5.** A project earns a bonus if it finishes early. The sponsor reassigns the strongest engineers to the critical tasks and removes every other duty from them, so that the early finish is certain. Which response to a positive risk is this?

- A. Enhance
- B. Exploit
- C. Share
- D. Accept

**6.** Which **two** responses apply to a positive risk (an opportunity)? **Choose two.**

- A. Mitigate
- B. Share
- C. Transfer
- D. Exploit
- E. Avoid

**7.** A task has five days of total float and slips by three days. It is not on the critical path. What is the effect on the project end date?

- A. The project finishes three days late
- B. The project finishes five days late
- C. None, because the delay is within the float
- D. The task becomes the new critical path and the end date moves

**8.** To pull the schedule in, the PM adds people to the tasks on the critical path and accepts the higher cost. Which technique is this?

- A. Fast tracking
- B. Lead
- C. Resource leveling
- D. Crashing

**9.** A task has an optimistic estimate of 4 days, a most likely estimate of 10 days and a pessimistic estimate of 22 days. Using the PERT formula, what is the expected duration?

- A. 11 days
- B. 12 days
- C. 10 days
- D. 13 days

**10.** The project has earned value of $40,000, actual cost of $50,000 and planned value of $45,000. What does this show?

- A. Under budget and ahead of schedule
- B. Over budget and ahead of schedule
- C. Under budget and behind schedule
- D. Over budget and behind schedule

**11.** The project team grows from 6 people to 9. How many additional communication channels does that create?

- A. 3
- B. 36
- C. 21
- D. 15

**12.** The scope of a software build is not yet clear, but the buyer needs the vendor to start work now. Which contract type fits best?

- A. Fixed price
- B. Time and material
- C. Maintenance agreement
- D. Master service agreement

**13.** Requirements are expected to change often. The team is small and cross-functional, and the customer wants to review working software every few weeks. Which approach fits best?

- A. Waterfall
- B. PRINCE2 with stage gates
- C. Agile
- D. A predictive plan with a fixed scope

**14.** Team members are arguing about who owns which tasks and are challenging the PM's decisions. Which stage of the team life cycle is this?

- A. Forming
- B. Performing
- C. Norming
- D. Storming

### 4.0 Basics of IT and Governance

**35.** A provider supplies finished software over the internet, usually by subscription, and the customer manages only its own data and users. Which service model is this?

- A. IaaS

- C. On-premises

**36.** The provider supplies virtual servers, storage and networking. The customer installs and manages the operating system and applications. Which service model is this?

- A. IaaS

**37.** The team will process names and email addresses of residents of the EU. The team and the company are based in the United States. What must the PM do?

- B. Remove all personal data from the project before the work begins

**40.** Every change that passes automated tests is released to production without a manual step. Which practice is this?

- A. Continuous integration
- B. Continuous deployment

### Answers

**1. A.** Never implement first. A request for "one small addition" goes through change control, so submit a change request and assess its impact. Only approved changes update the plan and baseline. See [1.3](01-concepts.md#13-change-control-process).

**2. B.** The order is: create the request, log it, preliminary review, impact assessment, recommendations, decision makers, CCB if applicable, then approval and implementation. See [1.3](01-concepts.md#13-change-control-process).

**3. C.** It has already happened, so it is an issue, not a risk. Risks are in the future. See [1.5](01-concepts.md#15-issue-management).

**4. D.** Insurance shifts the impact to a third party, which is transfer. Avoid would change the plan to remove the threat. See [1.4](01-concepts.md#14-risk-management).

**5. B.** Exploit makes sure the opportunity definitely happens. Enhance only raises its probability or impact. See [1.4](01-concepts.md#14-risk-management).

**6. B and D.** The positive responses are accept, enhance, exploit and share. Mitigate, transfer and avoid are for threats. See [1.4](01-concepts.md#14-risk-management).

**7. C.** A non-critical task can slip up to its float without moving the end date. Only when the float is used up does the task become critical. See [1.6](01-concepts.md#16-schedule-development-and-management).

**8. D.** Crashing adds resources to critical path tasks and costs money. Fast tracking overlaps tasks and adds risk. See [1.6](01-concepts.md#16-schedule-development-and-management).

**9. A.** PERT = (O + 4M + P) / 6 = (4 + 40 + 22) / 6 = 66 / 6 = 11 days. See [1.6](01-concepts.md#16-schedule-development-and-management).

**10. D.** CPI = EV / AC = 40,000 / 50,000 = 0.80, below 1, so over budget. SPI = EV / PV = 40,000 / 45,000 = 0.89, below 1, so behind schedule. See [1.7](01-concepts.md#17-quality-management-vs-performance-management).

**11. C.** Channels = n(n − 1) / 2. For 9 people that is 36, for 6 people it is 15, so the increase is 36 − 15 = 21. The trap is answering with 36. See [1.8](01-concepts.md#18-communication-management).

**12. B.** Unclear scope favors time and material (or cost plus). Fixed price needs a clear scope, because the seller carries the cost risk. See [1.11](01-concepts.md#111-procurement-and-vendor-selection).

**13. C.** Unclear and changing requirements, a small cross-functional team and frequent customer feedback point to Agile. Waterfall and other predictive approaches fix scope early. See [1.2](01-concepts.md#12-agile-vs-waterfall).

**14. D.** Storming is where conflict appears and roles and authority are tested. Forming is polite and unsure, norming is agreement, performing is high productivity. See [1.10](01-concepts.md#110-team-and-resource-management).

**15. A.** The charter authorizes the project and the PM, and the sponsor signs it. Nothing is baselined before it exists. See [2.2](02-lifecycle.md#22-initiation-phase).

**16. B.** The business case justifies the project before approval: problem, options, costs, benefits and risks. See [2.1](02-lifecycle.md#21-discovery-concept-preparation-artifacts).

**17. B.** Buying hardware is a capital expense. A monthly cloud subscription is an operating expense, expensed as it is incurred. See [2.1](02-lifecycle.md#21-discovery-concept-preparation-artifacts).

**18. D.** Every RACI row needs exactly one accountable person, who gives final sign-off. More than one blurs who decides. See [2.2](02-lifecycle.md#22-initiation-phase).

**19. A.** The WBS is a planning artifact. The charter, stakeholder identification and the preliminary scope statement all belong to initiation. See [2.2](02-lifecycle.md#22-initiation-phase) and [2.3](02-lifecycle.md#23-planning-phase).

**20. C.** The 100% rule says the WBS includes all the work and only the work in scope. It divides deliverables, not activities. See [2.3](02-lifecycle.md#23-planning-phase).

**21. D.** Collaborating means working together on a solution that meets everyone's needs, a win-win. It is usually the best answer unless the scenario describes an emergency. See [2.4](02-lifecycle.md#24-execution-phase).

**22. B.** Compromise means each side gives something up and both are partly satisfied. See [2.4](02-lifecycle.md#24-execution-phase).

**23. C.** Organizational change management covers training, communication, documentation and reinforcing adoption over time. See [2.4](02-lifecycle.md#24-execution-phase).

**24. A.** The usual order is: validate the deliverables, close contracts, remove access and release resources, then hold the closure meeting, archive records and obtain sign-off. See [2.5](02-lifecycle.md#25-closing-phase).

**25. B.** Formal acceptance and sign-off come from the customer or sponsor, not from the PM. See [2.5](02-lifecycle.md#25-closing-phase) and the [gaps](05-gaps.md).

**26. A.** ROI = net benefit / cost × 100% = 30,000 / 120,000 × 100% = 25%. See [2.1](02-lifecycle.md#21-discovery-concept-preparation-artifacts).

**27. C.** The critical path is found on a project network diagram. A Gantt chart shows schedule progress. See [3.1](03-tools.md#31-tools-used-throughout-the-project-life-cycle).

**28. D.** A Pareto chart sorts causes from largest to smallest with a cumulative line, which points at the vital few. See [3.3](03-tools.md#33-quality-and-performance-charts).

**29. B.** A fishbone diagram groups causes under branches such as people, process, equipment and materials to find root causes. See [3.3](03-tools.md#33-quality-and-performance-charts).

**30. D.** A control chart shows data against the mean and the upper and lower control limits, so it separates normal variation from special causes. See [3.3](03-tools.md#33-quality-and-performance-charts).

**31. C.** A burnup chart shows work completed against total scope, so a change in scope is visible. A burndown chart shows only work remaining. See [3.3](03-tools.md#33-quality-and-performance-charts).

**32. A.** The requirements traceability matrix links each requirement to its source, design, test and deliverable. See [3.1](03-tools.md#31-tools-used-throughout-the-project-life-cycle).

**33. B.** The risk register is the detailed log. The risk report is the summary of overall exposure and top risks for stakeholders. See [3.1](03-tools.md#31-tools-used-throughout-the-project-life-cycle).

**34. C.** A velocity chart shows the story points completed per sprint, which is what the team uses to forecast capacity. See [3.3](03-tools.md#33-quality-and-performance-charts).

**35. D.** SaaS is finished software delivered over the internet, usually by subscription. The more the provider supplies, the less the customer manages. See [4.4](04-it-governance.md#44-basic-it-concepts).

**36. A.** With IaaS the provider supplies virtual servers, storage and networking, and the customer manages the operating system and applications. See [4.4](04-it-governance.md#44-basic-it-concepts).

**37. D.** Privacy rules follow where the data subject lives, not where the company or the team sits. GDPR applies to EU residents. See [4.3](04-it-governance.md#43-compliance-and-privacy).

**38. A.** Remove access promptly when the project closes. Define access requirements at the start and remove them at the end. See [4.2](04-it-governance.md#42-information-security-concepts) and [2.5](02-lifecycle.md#25-closing-phase).

**39. C.** A change needs a tested rollback plan, an approval and a maintenance window, and is validated afterward. See [4.5](04-it-governance.md#45-operational-change-control-during-an-it-project).

**40. B.** Continuous deployment releases every passing change to production automatically. Continuous delivery keeps the code always releasable but a person still decides to release. See [4.5](04-it-governance.md#45-operational-change-control-during-an-it-project).
